One line. A warm game about an emergency locksmith: you open every invented lock with two thumbs, feeling and hearing what happens inside, and the whole game can be played without looking at the screen.

About (approx. 100 words). Someone is stuck outside their own door, and they call you. In Lockwright you see a photoreal cutaway of the mechanism, but the important part comes through your thumbs and your ears. A pin that binds pushes back and scrapes. A pin that sets gives a short, dry click. A spool pin pretends to set. Between jobs you buy and repair tools in the workshop, and every new tool opens a new kind of lock. "In the dark" mode turns the screen off: touch and sound are all you have. Haptics are never the only signal, so the game plays fine without them. Every lock is invented and unbranded. It's a game, not a course.

Features

  • Lock picking by feel: haptics, sound and image all describe the same physical object.
  • Photoreal cutaways of machined brass and steel, generated procedurally in Blender.
  • Several families of invented mechanisms, each asking for a different hand [1.0 list to be confirmed].
  • "In the dark" mode: the whole game is playable without looking.
  • A workshop instead of paid upgrades; spare picks in a leather tool roll instead of a health bar.
  • A showcase of short, sourced notes from the history of locks [1.0 scope to be confirmed].
  • Accessible from day one: three information channels, optional chalk marks on the cutaway, captions, touch targets ≥ 48 dp.

The honest part. The mechanisms are invented and simplified on purpose so you can read them. The game does not teach you to open real locks. A real locksmith always checks that the customer has the right to enter; so do you.
